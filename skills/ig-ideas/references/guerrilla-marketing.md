# Guerrilla Marketing for Instagram (ideas 67-70)

Unconventional stunts that earn attention in unexpected places, then get turned
into a repeatable system. The camera is the point: a stunt only counts if it
becomes content people share.

Two operating principles:

- **Think in stories, not campaigns.** People share a story worth retelling,
  not your funnel. Design the moment so the person who sees it wants to tell
  someone else.
- **Test small before going big.** A guerrilla idea is a hypothesis. Prove it
  on a handful of people before you spend real money.

## The 3-rule check

Physical gifts, mailers and installations must pass all three:

1. **Relevance.** It connects to your product, your message or something the
   recipient specifically cares about. A random branded mug is noise. A gift
   that makes a joke about their exact problem is a story.
2. **Relationship.** It opens or deepens a relationship. If it feels like a
   pitch, you spent money to annoy someone.
3. **Precision.** You know exactly who receives it and why. Unit cost is high,
   so it only works on a short, hand-qualified list.

## ROI discipline

A package or stunt costing real money sent to an unqualified person is waste.
Reserve high-cost plays for named creators, partners and customers where one
result pays for the batch. Send to a handful first and measure replies, tags,
Stories and sales. Scale only the version that earns responses.

## Patterns that fit Instagram

- **Seed to creators, not to everyone.** A thoughtful item to 20 micro-creators
  with a real overlap in audience, with no required post. The best ones post
  because it is worth posting.
- **Make the unboxing the content.** Design the package so opening it is a
  Reel the recipient wants to film. Reply to every tag.
- **Stage the stunt where the camera is.** Pick a place with foot traffic and
  film it from three angles. The video is the campaign, the physical act is the
  prop.
- **QR to a specific Reel or offer.** Never the generic profile. Track scans.
- **Turn customers into the cast.** Feature a real customer moment with
  permission rather than staging one.
- **Piggyback an event without a booth.** Be visibly useful around a larger
  event your audience attends, and film it.
- **Branded content people actually want to watch.** An original short film or
  series entertains first and sells second.

## Case patterns (describe the pattern, verify any figures before quoting)

- **Competitor-jab stunt at an industry event.** A physical gag aimed at a
  real weakness in a rival, staged where the audience gathers.
- **Skywriting over a conference.** Attention taken from above an event the
  brand did not sponsor.
- **A live spectacle with huge concurrent viewers.** A story so large it
  dwarfs any ad buy.
- **Mass-mailed food to target accounts.** A precision-targeted, story-worthy
  gift program with measured conversion.
- **Personalized memos to prospects.** Treating each recipient as an audience
  of one.
- **Collectible cards of the team and community.** Making the brand fun to
  share.
- **A branded GIF library.** The brand rides along inside other people's
  conversations. Note that Instagram Stories offer a GIF sticker search, so
  this pattern still works there.
- **Original branded film.** Entertainment-first content that travels further
  than an ad.

Some case figures circulate without a primary source. Check before repeating
any number to a client.

## How to run one

1. **Pick the story.** What would a target retell? Start from the story, then
   choose the tactic.
2. **Qualify the list.** Name the people. Confirm the value justifies the cost.
3. **Run the 3-rule check.**
4. **Plan the capture.** Who films, which three shots, and what the caption is.
5. **Test small.** Ship to a handful. Measure.
6. **Turn the stunt into a system.** If it works, make it a recurring program.
