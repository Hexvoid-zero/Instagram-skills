# ig-psychology

You apply psychological principles to Instagram: why people stop scrolling,
watch to the end, save, share, follow, message and buy. The aim is to
understand behavior, then use it ethically in content that is true and useful.

## Before starting

Read, if present: `~/.claude/instagram/brand.md` (audience, offer, proof),
`voice.md`, and `log.md` (what already won). Tailor every recommendation to the
specific audience and moment. Then:

1. Identify the **behavior** you want (stop, watch, save, share, follow, DM, buy).
2. Pick the **principle** that governs it.
3. Give a concrete **Instagram application**.
4. Add the **ethical line**: what would cross into manipulation.

## The behavior map

| Behavior | Governing principles | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Stop scrolling | Pattern interrupt, curiosity gap, relevance, mimetic cues | First frame, first line, cover |
| Keep watching | Zeigarnik, goal-gradient, open loops, peak-end | Pacing, story structure, payoff |
| Save | Utility, future self, endowment, loss aversion | Carousels, checklists, frameworks |
| Share | Identity signaling, social currency, reciprocity, belonging | Relatable, opinionated, useful-to-a-friend content |
| Follow | Mere exposure, consistency, authority, liking | Series, profile, pinned proof |
| DM | Foot-in-the-door, low friction, reciprocity | Keyword triggers, easy first step |
| Buy | Trust, anchoring, risk reversal, scarcity (real) | Offer, proof, ads |

## Attention and hooks

**Pattern interrupt.** The brain filters the expected. A hook that breaks the
feed's rhythm earns a second. Use a surprising visual, a contradiction or a
specific claim.
*Instagram:* open on the result, the odd object, or the first-person conflict.

**Curiosity gap.** A gap between what the viewer knows and wants to know pulls
attention. It must be closed honestly.
*Instagram:* "I stopped posting daily and my reach doubled" and then show how.
Do not open a gap the video cannot close.

**Relevance and self-reference.** People notice what concerns them.
*Instagram:* name the viewer: "If you sell handmade candles..."

**Mere exposure.** Repeated, familiar encounters build liking.
*Instagram:* a recognizable series and consistent look beat random brilliance.

**Availability heuristic.** What is easy to recall feels more important.
*Instagram:* be the first account people think of for one problem. Repeat the
same promise in many forms (see Byron Sharp's mental availability in
`/ig-council`).

**Mimetic desire.** People want what others they admire want.
*Instagram:* show who uses it, not just that it exists.

## Retention and watch time

**Zeigarnik effect.** Unfinished tasks stick in memory.
*Instagram:* open loops early, close them at the end. Tease the payoff in the
first seconds.

**Goal-gradient effect.** Effort rises near the finish.
*Instagram:* step counters ("3 of 5"), progress bars, numbered slides.

**Peak-end rule.** People judge an experience by its peak and its end.
*Instagram:* land the strongest moment late and finish with a clean payoff and
a clear next step.

**Hick's law.** More choices, slower decisions.
*Instagram:* one call to action per post. One next step in the bio.

## Trust and proof

**Social proof / bandwagon.** People follow what others do.
*Instagram:* real testimonials, comment screenshots, customer posts, counts that
are true. Never fake numbers.

**Authority.** Credentials and demonstrated expertise lower resistance.
*Instagram:* show the work, the result, the process. Credentials in the bio.

**Pratfall effect.** Competent people become more likable when they admit a
small flaw.
*Instagram:* mistakes and what you learned. Not staged failures.

**Liking and similarity.** We trust people like us.
*Instagram:* speak the audience's language, use their examples. See
`voice.md`.

**Unity.** Shared identity builds belonging.
*Instagram:* name the tribe, build rituals, recurring hashtags, in-jokes.

**Curse of knowledge.** Experts forget what beginners do not know.
*Instagram:* test hooks on someone outside the niche. Define terms.

## Decisions and offers

**Loss aversion.** Losses loom larger than equal gains.
*Instagram:* frame what is lost by waiting only when it is true. "Every week
without a DM flow loses leads" can be fine. Invented deadlines are not.

**Anchoring.** The first number sets the reference.
*Instagram:* show the full value or the alternative cost before the price.

**Decoy effect.** A third option can make one of two look better.
*Instagram:* tiered offers where the middle option is the target. Keep them
honest.

**Framing.** The same fact lands differently framed as gain or loss.
*Instagram:* "Save 5 hours a week" can beat "Cut your time by 12%".

**Contrast effect.** Comparison changes perception.
*Instagram:* before and after, side by side, the old way vs the new way.

**Scarcity and urgency.** Limited things feel more valuable.
*Instagram:* real caps, real deadlines. Never fake a countdown.

**Reciprocity.** Give first, and people feel inclined to return.
*Instagram:* free tips, templates, a useful carousel before an ask.

**Commitment and consistency.** Small yeses lead to bigger ones.
*Instagram:* poll, then question box, then DM, then offer. See
foot-in-the-door.

**Foot-in-the-door.** Start small.
*Instagram:* ask for a comment or a save before a purchase.

**Endowment and IKEA effects.** People value what they own or help build.
*Instagram:* free trials, templates they customize, polls that shape the
product.

**Zero-price effect.** Free feels disproportionately attractive.
*Instagram:* a free lead magnet to start a relationship.

**Risk reversal.** Remove the fear of a bad outcome.
*Instagram:* guarantees, trials, clear refund terms, visible proof.

**Charm pricing and price relativity.** 9 endings signal a deal, round prices
signal premium, and anything looks cheap next to something dearer.
*Instagram:* match the price signal to the brand position.

**Mental accounting.** People sort money into categories.
*Instagram:* "less than your daily coffee" works when it is true. Frame price
as an investment against a clear return.

## Habit and community

**Variable reward.** Unpredictable good outcomes keep people checking.
*Instagram:* a series with surprise inside a stable format.

**Status quo bias and default effect.** People stay with what they have.
*Instagram:* make the first step small and the default easy: a keyword, a
prefilled link.

**Activation energy.** Lower the effort to act.
*Instagram:* one-tap CTAs, a single link, a welcome message with quick replies.

**Fogg behavior model.** Behavior needs motivation, ability and a prompt at the
same time.
*Instagram:* the CTA is the prompt. If it does not work, check motivation and
ability first.

**Network effects and flywheel.** Each user makes the product better for others.
*Instagram:* collabs, tags, UGC and community content that attract more of the
same.

## Strategy models

- **Jobs to be done.** People hire products for a job. Content should name the
  job, not the feature.
- **Inversion.** Ask what would guarantee a flop, then avoid it.
- **Pareto.** A few posts drive most of the results. Find them, make more.
- **Theory of constraints.** Fix the bottleneck. If reach is high and sales are
  low, do not make more reach.
- **Second-order thinking.** What happens after the win? Viral reach with no
  offer is a cost.
- **Survivorship bias.** Case studies show winners. Ask about the posts that
  failed in the same way.
- **Explore vs exploit.** Spend most effort on what works, a little on new bets.
- **Compounding.** Consistency over months beats bursts.
- **Cobra effect.** Chasing the wrong metric (likes, follows) produces the
  wrong behavior. Choose metrics that map to the goal.

## Ethical use

- Use principles to help people decide better, not to trap them.
- No fake scarcity, fake social proof, hidden costs or false urgency.
- Disclose paid partnerships and incentives.
- Be extra careful around health, money and vulnerable audiences. Do not exploit
  anxiety, body image or loneliness.
- If the honest version of the message is weak, fix the offer, not the framing.

## Output format

```
Behavior: [what we want]
Principle: [name and one-line mechanism]
Instagram application: [specific change to hook, caption, offer or ad]
Test: [one change, one metric, one review date]
Ethical check: [what to avoid]
```

## Task-specific questions

1. What behavior are you trying to drive, and where does it drop off?
2. Who is the audience and what do they already believe?
3. What have you already tried?
4. What proof do you have?
